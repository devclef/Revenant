# Tone and Visual Voice

Revenant is the ghost of your past self returning with the receipts. The mood is a letter found in an attic: wax seals, faded ink, the quiet weight of time. It is spooky the way a candlelit room is spooky, not the way a haunted house ride is.

## The feeling

- **Quiet, unhurried, a little uncanny.** The past is speaking. Let it.
- **Respectful.** Users are confronting their own memories, sometimes painful ones. Never mock them for misremembering.
- **Plain-spoken.** Atmosphere comes from design and restraint, not from wordplay.

## Copy

- Short sentences. Second person. Calm.
- Light, consistent metaphor: *seal*, *sealed*, *open*, *unseal*, *letter*, *record*, *then* and *now*. Use these as product vocabulary everywhere.
- Good: "This letter was sealed on 30 October 2026. Before you read it, tell us how you remember that autumn."
- Good: "Your seal is intact. Nothing here has changed since you wrote it."
- Avoid: puns ("boo-tiful", "spooktacular"), skulls, blood, gore, jump scares, "mwahaha", horror clichés, exclamation marks.
- Data copy stays literal and neutral (see `neutrality.md`). Atmosphere never bends a number or adds a judgement.
- Errors and empty states stay in voice but stay clear: say what happened and what the user can do.

## Visual direction

- Parchment and paper textures, deep ink colours, wax-seal reds and muted golds, plenty of negative space.
- Typography: a serif or hand-set feel for letters and headings, a clean readable face for data and UI.
- "Faded ink" is an effect on decorative elements only. Body text and all numbers must meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.
- The reveal is the centrepiece: a deliberate, ceremonial unsealing sequence. It must be skippable and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- No colour coding that implies a number moved in a good or bad direction.

## Moments that matter

- **Sealing:** feels final and a little solemn. Make it clear the letter can't be changed after this.
- **Waiting:** the sealed letter is visible but unreadable, like an envelope on a mantelpiece.
- **Recall:** gentle prompts, no hints about what's inside.
- **Reveal:** memory first, then the letter, then the numbers. Let each land before the next.
- **Locking:** the passkey prompt is the wax seal being pressed. Warnings about lost keys stay plain and unmissable. Atmosphere never softens a warning that could cost the user their letter.
- **Cards:** the letter card and backup key card are printable keepsakes in the same visual language. The backup key card says clearly what it is, that it opens the letter on its own after the opening date, and that it should be kept somewhere safe.
- **Countdown:** scanning early shows a quiet "still sealed" state and the time remaining. Nothing about the contents.
